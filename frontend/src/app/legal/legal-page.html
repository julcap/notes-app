Privacy policy

Last updated September 18, 2026

Publication draft. Operator and contact details are pending publication approval and legal review. Google, Facebook, and Amazon OAuth production approval is still pending. This page describes the current application behavior; it does not mean those external approvals are complete.

Scope

This policy describes information processed by the Minutes meeting-notes application. It covers account, note, attachment, sharing, session, notification, security, and diagnostic features implemented in this repository.

Information the service processes

  • Account and profile: email address, an optional display name, email-verification state, pending email changes, sign-in provider, notification preferences, and account timestamps. Local passwords are stored as password hashes rather than readable passwords.
  • Social sign-in: when selected, Google, Facebook, or Amazon supplies a provider account identifier, email address, name, and available email-verification claim under the requested profile scopes. Minutes stores the provider and provider account identifier needed to recognize the sign-in.
  • Security: encrypted authenticator secrets, hashed one-time backup codes, hashed session and email tokens, session creation and last-use times, browser user-agent, peer or trusted-proxy-derived IP address, and rate-limit counters. Active-session device and IP descriptions are approximate.
  • Meeting content: titles, note bodies, attendees, meeting and scheduled dates, action items, owners, due dates, and completion state. Search indexes are derived from note content.
  • Attachments: uploaded bytes plus filename, size, detected or supplied content type, storage key, and upload time. Each attachment is limited to 20 MiB.
  • Sharing: the recipient account, recipient email and display name shown to the owner, view or edit permission, sharing time, and prior-recipient contact records used only when the owner explicitly confirms re-sharing.
  • Notifications: opt-in reminder and weekly-digest settings, scheduling information, and delivery keys used to avoid duplicate email. Reminder and digest content can include note titles, scheduled times, and open action items.
  • Operational signals: request method, matched route template, response status, duration, request ID, aggregate counters, and scrubbed error type and code locations. Structured logs omit raw paths, query strings, request bodies, headers, cookies, IP addresses, emails, note identifiers, and note content.

How information is used

Information is used to create and secure accounts; authenticate sessions; store, search, display, export, and delete meeting records; deliver authorized attachments; apply sharing permissions; send email verification, recovery, reminders, and opted-in digests; enforce rate limits; and diagnose service errors.

When information leaves the service

  • People you choose to share a meeting with can access its content and attachments. An editor can change the meeting, action items, and attachments; a viewer can read and download them. The owner controls and can revoke that access.
  • Google, Facebook, or Amazon processes a social sign-in when you choose that provider. Their own privacy terms govern information they process.
  • An email delivery provider receives the destination email and transactional message needed to send verification, recovery, reminder, or digest email.
  • Attachment bytes may be stored by the configured S3-compatible storage provider. The application can instead use local storage; the selected live provider is an operator deployment decision.
  • Sentry receives scrubbed technical error events only if an operator configures a valid DSN. Error tracking is disabled by default, sends no default PII, and disables tracing, profiling, replay, and log forwarding.
  • The frontend currently loads typography from Google Fonts, so a browser can disclose ordinary request metadata such as its IP address, user-agent, and referring page to Google when fetching those files.

The application does not include advertising trackers, a data-sale feature, or a cookie-consent tracker.

Storage, security, and retention

Account and meeting records are stored in PostgreSQL. Attachment bytes use local or S3-compatible object storage. Access tokens expire after 15 minutes. Refresh sessions expire after 24 hours, or up to 30 days when “Remember me” is selected, and can be revoked individually or all at once.

Deleting a meeting hides it immediately and provides a server-enforced undo window of 15 seconds. Deleted meetings and their attachments are scheduled for permanent purge after 30 days; actual removal depends on the purge job running successfully.

Account deletion is immediate in the application: it hard-deletes the account, its owned meetings, attachments, sessions, verification records, and social identities with no application undo window. Content owned by someone else is not deleted merely because a collaborator deletes their account.

Copies in operational backups may retain earlier data after an application deletion until those backups expire or are rotated. No backup retention period or deletion-from-backup guarantee has been approved for publication, and a restored backup can temporarily reintroduce data that operations must reconcile.

Your controls

Account settings lets you update your profile, opt in or out of reminders and weekly digests, review and revoke active sessions, manage two-factor authentication, and permanently delete your account. Meeting owners can export notes, revoke collaborators, delete meetings, and use the 15-second undo action.

Contact and changes

No operator name, postal address, privacy contact, or legally approved policy-change process is currently published. Those details must be supplied and reviewed before this draft is used for a public launch or an OAuth production-review submission.

Terms of service

Last updated September 18, 2026

1. The service

Minutes provides account-based meeting notes, attachments, action items, search, export, reminders, session controls, and owner-managed collaboration. These terms describe the implemented service while final operator-specific legal terms remain under review.

2. Your account

Provide an email address you control, keep sign-in credentials and backup codes secure, and review active sessions if a device is lost or shared. You are responsible for actions taken through your account until its sessions are revoked. Social sign-in also depends on the selected provider's account and terms.

3. Your content and sharing

You keep responsibility for the notes and files you upload, including having permission to record, store, and share them. Do not upload content that is unlawful or infringes another person's rights.

A meeting owner may grant another verified Minutes account permission to view or edit a meeting. Viewers can read and download its attachments. Editors can also change content, action items, and attachments. Only the owner can delete the meeting or manage sharing. Revoking access stops later service access but cannot retract copies a recipient already exported or downloaded.

4. Acceptable use

Do not use the service to break the law, harm others, upload malware, probe or bypass security controls, access another person's data without permission, interfere with service operation, or evade rate limits. Attachment uploads are limited to 20 MiB per file.

5. Export, deletion, and retention

Owners can export a meeting as Markdown or PDF. Meeting deletion has a server-enforced 15-second undo window, then remains hidden and is scheduled for permanent purge after 30 days. Permanently deleting an account removes the account and everything it owns immediately from the live application, with no undo.

Operational backups can retain earlier copies until normal rotation. A specific backup-retention period and restore reconciliation process have not yet been approved for publication.

6. External services

Depending on operator configuration and your choices, the service can use Google, Facebook, or Amazon for sign-in; an email delivery provider; S3-compatible attachment storage; Google Fonts; and scrubbed, disabled-by-default Sentry error reporting. Those providers apply their own terms and can change or discontinue their services.

7. Availability and changes

No service level or support commitment has been published. Final legal review must define any warranty, liability, service-change, suspension, termination, and notice terms before public launch; this draft does not invent those commitments.

8. Operator and legal contact

Operator identity, legal contact, governing law, and venue are pending publication approval. These terms must not be presented as final, or used to claim OAuth production readiness, until those fields and the full text have received legal review.
